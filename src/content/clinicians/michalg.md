## _Therapy Approach for Teens and Adults_

_I believe that meaningful change begins within the context of a genuine therapeutic relationship. My approach is relational, psychodynamic, trauma-informed, and collaborative. Together, we explore not only current challenges and symptoms but also the deeper emotional experiences, attachment patterns, and family-of-origin relationships that have shaped how you relate to yourself and others._

_I strive to create a warm, thoughtful, and nonjudgmental space where clients feel safe to slow down, make sense of their experiences, and reconnect with parts of themselves that may have been overlooked or protected. Therapy is not only about reducing distress, it is also about cultivating greater self-understanding, emotional freedom, resilience, and more fulfilling relationships._

_While my work is grounded in contemporary psychodynamic and attachment-based therapy, I thoughtfully integrate evidence-based approaches—including EMDR, CBT, DBT, Functional Analytic Psychotherapy (FAP), and narrative therapy-when they can support each client's unique goals and needs. Rather than following a predetermined formula, I tailor therapy to the individual, honoring each person's history, culture, strengths, and capacity for growth_

## About Michal

**Michal** is a licensed clinical social worker who has been providing psychotherapy to adolescents, adults, couples, families, and groups for over two decades. She earned her bachelor's degree in Social Work from Tel Aviv University in Israel and her Master of Social Work from the University of Washington.

Since establishing her private practice in Seattle in 2016, Michal has specialized in working with trauma, complex trauma, life transitions, identity, relationships, parenting, neurodivergence, and the impact of social, cultural, and systemic experiences on emotional well-being.

In 2026, Michal completed the two-year **Psychoanalytic Psychotherapy Program** at the Seattle Psychoanalytic Society and Institute (SPSI). She continues to deepen her clinical work through ongoing psychoanalytic consultation and participation in a weekly Modern Psychoanalytic Lens consultation group.

Michal's therapeutic approach is primarily relational and psychodynamic, grounded in attachment theory and informed by contemporary psychoanalytic thinking. She believes that healing occurs through a collaborative therapeutic relationship where clients can safely explore longstanding patterns, deepen self-understanding, process trauma, and create meaningful and lasting change.

Her work is also informed by evidence-based approaches including Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR), Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), Functional Analytic Psychotherapy (FAP), narrative therapy, and anti-oppressive psychotherapy. She integrates these approaches thoughtfully and flexibly, tailoring therapy to each client's unique needs rather than relying on a one-size-fits-all model.

Michal practices from a culturally humble, feminist, trauma-informed, and anti-oppressive perspective. She welcomes clients of all racial, cultural, ethnic, religious, gender, sexual orientation, and neurodivergent identities, recognizing that our personal experiences are shaped not only by our individual histories but also by our relationships, families, communities, and broader social contexts.

She is also a Certified Gottman Educator for both **Bringing Baby Home®**, supporting expecting and new parents, and **The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work®**, helping couples strengthen their relationships.
