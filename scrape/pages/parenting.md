Parenting can strain even strong relationships. Navigating daily pressures like disagreements over discipline and routines, managing exhaustion and stress, finding time for connection, balancing individual needs with family demands can significantly impact our mental and emotional health.

Family patterns and parental influences also shape relationship dynamics. This includes working through inherited communication styles, unresolved family conflicts, different expectations based on upbringing, and attachment patterns formed in childhood. By recognizing these influences, parents can develop healthier ways of relating to each other."

## _Our Approach_

-   Communication skills
    
-   Understanding learned behaviors
    
-   Major life changes including new siblings, moving, school changes, family loss.
    
-   Emotion regulation and co-regulation
    
-   Routine building and clear expectations

-   CBT (Cognitive Behavior Therapy) — Identifies and changes negative thought patterns
    
-   **Behavior Management:** Understanding the function of the behavior and supporting the child to find healthier ways to meet his/her needs.
    
-   **Neurodiversity Support:** Strategies for children with ADHD, autism (ASD) and sensory sensitivities.
    
-   **DBT** (Dialectical Behavior Therapy) Informed — Distress tolerance, emotion regulation, interpersonal effectiveness, and mindfulness practices

-   Separation and divorced household
    
-   Co-parenting
    
-   Difficulty trusting others or feeling safe in relationships
    
-   Caregiver burnout, stress and obstacles
    
-   Generational patterns and trauma

**Attachment-Based Therapy** — Addresses parenting rooted in relational patterns

**Psychodynamic Approaches** — Explores deeper roots of anxiety and stress in parenting

**Somatic Techniques** — Manages the physical symptoms of trauma focusing on the nervous system and physical sensations

**Psychoeducation** —Provides learning in the nature of struggles with parenting and how treatment works.
